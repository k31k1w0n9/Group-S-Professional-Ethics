## 4.3 Food Descriptions, Allergens, and Food Safety

**Allergen rules and practice in Malaysia.** The Food Act 1983 and Food Regulations 1985 govern food quality and labelling. The Regulations require ingredients known to cause hypersensitivity to be declared. Soon (2018) studied allergen labelling on packaged products sold in Malaysia, including “may contain” statements, and called for clearer guidance. In food service, Tan et al. (2023) found that restaurant managers’ and servers’ knowledge of food allergies was low compared with Western studies. Servers were less aware than managers of allergy practices and training needs. These studies cover packaged food and restaurants, not app listings, so we do not assume their findings apply to ReMeal.

**Allergen information in online ordering.** The closest evidence is a UK Food Standards Agency study (Koidis et al., 2024). People with food hypersensitivities rated the information on online ordering platforms slightly negatively and raised concerns about cross-contact and outdated information. In a pilot, eleven meals sold as gluten-free or suitable for milk or peanut allergy were lab-tested. One contained 2,365 mg/kg of gluten, far above the 20 mg/kg limit for gluten-free claims. The sample was small, self-selected and UK-based, so the results cannot be generalised to ReMeal. Such gaps still matter because severe allergic reactions can be life-threatening (Valenta et al., 2015).

**Photos.** We found no peer-reviewed Malaysian study of how often app photos match the food delivered, so we cannot say how common mismatches are. We do not call ReMeal’s photos inaccurate unless we can verify it.

## 5.3 Food Descriptions and Photos

On 1 November 2026, we reviewed two listings from each of two stores in the ReMeal app: [Dorsett Hartamas Kuala Lumpur](https://remeal.app/stores/dorsett-hartamas-kuala-lumpur) and [Krem Tarik, Kota Damansara](https://remeal.app/stores/krem-tarik-kota-damansara). For each listing, we recorded the contents, ingredients, price, availability, collection rules, allergen information and photos.

**Table 1. Details given in the listings reviewed**

| Listing | Details given | Not stated |
|---|---|---|
| Dorsett: 40% OFF Buffet Breakfast (Available Daily), RM 27.00 (was RM 45.00) | Dine-in only; advance booking allowed; “local and international dishes”, a children’s menu; kids below 5 eat free; “50 left”; available 06:30–10:00 | Dish names, ingredients, allergens |
| Dorsett: 50% OFF Weekend Buffet Hi-tea (Saturdays), RM 79.00 (was RM 158.00) | Dine-in only; advance booking, redeemed on that week’s Saturday; 13:00–17:00; “50 left”; “local favorites and international flavors” | Dish names, ingredients, allergens |
| Krem Tarik: Mystery Single Scoop – Eternal Series, RM 6.00 (was RM 11.00) | “A scoop of surprise” from four named flavours (Chocolate, Vanilla, Dark Choc Mint, Popping Lime); “Contains dairy”; “3 left”; counter collection 12:00–22:00; dine-in on request | Flavour received; scoop size; other allergens |
| Krem Tarik: Waffle + Eternal Series, RM 8.00 (was RM 12.00) | “One golden waffle” with a mystery scoop (same four flavours); “Contains dairy”; “1 left” | Flavour received; waffle contents; other allergens |
(Fig. A21-A24)

**The level of detail varies.** Dorsett describes its buffets only in general terms and names no dish. Its breakfast availability line shows 06:30–10:00, but the description says “6.30am – 10.30am”, and the listing does not explain the difference. Krem Tarik names the four possible flavours, so the uncertainty of the mystery item is limited to a known range. It does not, however, give a scoop size or say what the waffle contains.

**Photos.** Dorsett’s photos show an arranged breakfast spread (cereal, fruit, toast, jam, bacon, eggs, juice) and a buffet of traditional dishes and desserts. Because no dishes are named, we cannot compare the photos with what is served. Krem Tarik’s scoop photo can only be an example because the flavour is a mystery, but the listing does not say so. The waffle photo also shows a green drink that the description does not mention, so customers may not know whether it is included. It may be styling. We do not claim any photo is inaccurate.

**Prices, stock and sustainability claims.** All four listings show a crossed-out original price and a “left” count (50, 50, 3 and 1). The store pages show sales and CO₂e figures (5,019 kg for Dorsett and 116 kg for Krem Tarik), and the Dorsett page says the customer has “saved food from being wasted”. The listings do not say what surplus is being sold. We could not verify the prices, the counts or the figures, or how the figures are calculated. Low counts such as “1 left” may create a sense of urgency. We treat these as transparency questions, not proven misleading claims.

**Clear conditions.** Both stores state clearly how the food is received. Dorsett is dine-in only, with advance booking and a redemption day. Krem Tarik uses counter collection, with dine-in on request.

**Ethical significance.** Customers decide what to buy using only what the listing shows. Clear conditions and named flavour ranges support honest, informed choice, while vague descriptions, the time mismatch and the unexplained drink weaken it. The mystery format is a disclosed trade-off: the customer accepts uncertainty for a lower price and less waste. It is more acceptable when the range is named, as Krem Tarik does. These points relate to Code Clauses 1.06 and 3.01 on truthful statements and making important trade-offs clear (see Section 6.2). Listings could name typical buffet dishes, say what each photo includes, give the size of mystery items, fix the time mismatch and explain what surplus is offered.

## 5.4 Allergen and Food Safety Information

ReMeal’s Terms state that the platform is not required to provide ingredient or allergen information and does not guarantee that vendors’ goods are allergen-free. Customers with allergies or dietary requirements are told to contact the vendor before ordering (ReMeal, 2024b, § 5.3; Appendix, Fig. A10). The Terms also say ReMeal is not liable for vendors’ or customers’ acts or omissions in providing the goods. They do not guarantee that vendor listings are accurate or complete (ReMeal, 2024b, § 9.3; Appendix, Fig. A14).

The listings follow no consistent format for allergens. Both Krem Tarik listings state “Contains dairy”. Neither Dorsett listing mentions allergens, even though the breakfast includes a children’s menu. We cannot tell whether “Contains dairy” is complete: the waffle’s ingredients are not stated, and waffles are usually made with wheat and egg. A missing statement does not show that Dorsett’s food contains allergens, only that the listing does not say. These gaps make it harder for customers with allergies to choose suitable food (Table 1; Appendix, Fig. A17). We see this as the most serious gap because it risks harm to customers with food hypersensitivities, to whom the platform and its vendors owe a duty of care. Even a standard line such as “allergen information not provided, please ask the store” would make the gap visible.

Contacting the vendor does not fully solve this. Requests may be misunderstood, and food prepared for a customer’s needs could still be exposed to allergens through cross-contact. Gaps in Malaysian restaurant staff’s allergy knowledge (Tan et al., 2023) make clear communication and safe handling more important. The Terms leave open who should confirm allergen information and what happens if it is wrong.

**Limitations.** Four listings from two stores (a hotel buffet and an ice-cream shop) on one date do not represent ReMeal’s listings in general, such as bakery or supermarket items. The listings may have changed since then. We reviewed the screens only and did not buy or attend, so we cannot say whether the food matched the listings. We also did not check whether other parts of the app show allergen information.
