## 4.2 Refunds and Consumer Protection

Malaysia’s Consumer Protection (Electronic Trade Transaction) Regulations 2024 replaced the 2012 rules. They require seller disclosures, error-correction mechanisms and complaint channels, and address re-delivery costs for materially different or defective goods; they do not guarantee refunds for every complaint (regs. 3, 5–7).

Abenoh et al. (2019) discuss overlapping laws protecting online-food consumers without measuring refund outcomes. In contrast, Siali et al. (2024) surveyed 310 Sarawak users and found a positive but statistically insignificant relationship between redress and loyalty. Most respondents were students using larger platforms. Legal protections and customer perceptions therefore provide complementary questions, but neither establishes ReMeal’s remedies.

## 5.2 Refunds and Cancellations

ReMeal’s Terms allow customers to request cancellation through support before vendor acceptance. Customer-requested cancellation after acceptance receives no refund (§§ 5.7–5.8; A15). Vendors may decline special instructions when compliance is not feasible or commercially reasonable, without a refund or replacement for non-compliance with those instructions (§ 5.2).

Collection terms require inspection before leaving and allow refusal of refunds for unreasonable customer delay (§§ 7.2.1–7.2.2). Eligible refunds return through the original payment method, subject to supporting information (ReMeal, 2024b).

Other remedies remain possible: the seller guide describes full or partial refunds for unfulfilled orders and support-processed refunds for missing items after completion (ReMeal, n.d.-d). The sales guide lists Pending, Open, Collected and Cancelled, but no separate Accepted status (ReMeal, n.d.-c), leaving the customer’s cancellation cutoff worth investigating.

On 3 November, our iOS delivery checkout displayed an RM16 item, RM13.55 delivery and RM29.55 total. No cancellation warning was visible in the captured screens before “Proceed to Payment”; an out-of-stock option was set to replacement by default (A18–A20). We did not enter payment or test refunds.

Acceptance restrictions may protect vendors from waste, but customers need clear conditions before committing. Clauses 1.06 and 3.01 support honest communication and visible trade-offs. Part 2 will examine payment-stage disclosures, acceptance status and complaint routes; the current evidence does not establish deception.
