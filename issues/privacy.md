## 4.1 Privacy and Personal Data in Food Delivery Apps

Ebrahimi et al. (2021) mapped 59 software-engineering studies, mostly addressing leak detection. Consent research examines a different weakness: 74% of 543 participants skipped a fictitious social network’s privacy policy (Obar & Oeldorf-Hirsch, 2020), while only 11.8% of 680 UK websites using five consent platforms met Nouwens et al.’s (2020) minimum criteria. This was not a representative website sample.

Al Rezan et al. (2025) found nine Malaysian food apps failed standards the authors developed. Together, these studies support checking disclosures and interfaces alongside technical risks. Context shapes privacy decisions (Acquisti et al., 2015), and location raises distinctive concerns (Keßler & McKenzie, 2018). None establishes a ReMeal violation.

## 5.1 Privacy and Personal Data

ReMeal’s notice permits collecting contact, location, transaction, device and message data, sharing with vendors and service providers, and overseas transfers (ReMeal, 2024a, §§ 1, 3–5; Figs. A6–A7, A9).

Sign-in links account creation with agreement to the Terms and Privacy Policy, without a separate consent control visible on that screen; guest access is offered (Fig. A1). The Terms treat platform use as consent and direct deletion requests to email (ReMeal, 2024b, §§ 1.0, 2.2; Figs. A4–A5). The account form remains unchecked.

The Terms require a location to show nearby vendors (ReMeal, 2024b, § 5.5; Fig. A10), while the app presents device access as an enhancement. Providing a location and granting device access are different, so this wording does not establish a contradiction. Selecting “Set From Map” triggered Android’s permission prompt; backing out enabled “Use Default Location” and access to the home screen (Figs. A2–A3, A12). We did not test ordering from it.

Apple’s developer label lists identity-linked data (Apple App Store, n.d.; Fig. A16). Google Play reports data “isn’t encrypted” (ReMeal, n.d.-b; Fig. A8); this field concerns transmission, not storage (Google Play, n.d.). These are developer claims. Our concern is understandable, meaningful data choice; we found no evidence of misuse.
